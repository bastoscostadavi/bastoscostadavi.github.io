Davi Bastos Costa

Physicist bridging theoretical physics and AI.

I am a Schmidt AI in Science Postdoctoral Fellow at the University of Chicago, working on AI for scientific discovery with Chenhao Tan at the Chicago Human + AI Lab and with James Evans at Knowledge Lab.

I completed my PhD in theoretical physics at the University of Chicago under Clay Córdova, with a thesis on non-invertible symmetries in finite-group gauge theories. That work generalizes symmetry beyond group theory: the symmetries of a quantum field theory are realized by topological defects, and these defects need not be invertible. I was awarded the Nathan Sugarman Award for Excellence in Graduate Research for this line of work, and the University of Chicago nominated me for the Schmidt Science Fellows program. Alongside it, I worked with Dam Thanh Son on effective field theory for weakly bound two-neutron halo nuclei — an appealing detour, since it applies machinery built for relativistic particle physics to non-relativistic nuclei with a direct line to experiment.

Earlier, I did my master's at the University of São Paulo with Gustavo Burdman, on dark matter and neutrino masses in twin Higgs models, and spent 2019 at Fermilab's Theory Division with Roni Harnik. During my undergraduate years, also at USP, I worked with Nestor Caticha on statistical mechanics of neural networks and emergent collective behavior in social systems — the first thread of what I do now. My earliest published results were on anomaly cancellation: a general solution to the U(1) anomaly equations, highlighted by the Brazilian Physical Society.

My current research studies large language models as a statistical-physics problem, and my broader interest is in computational social science: using formal models and large-scale data to understand collective behavior, whether the agents involved are people or machines. Before Chicago, I was a postdoctoral fellow at USP's TELUS Digital Research Hub with Renato Vicente, working on emergence in multi-agent systems based on LLMs. Three questions organize the work: whether multi-agent LLM systems admit analytical descriptions (the Mini-Mafia benchmark, where a closed-form expression predicts collective outcomes from three per-model capabilities), how persona conditioning shapes model behavior (moral robustness and susceptibility as measurable properties of a model's persona machinery), and what emergent misalignment does to that machinery (persona-model collapse).
